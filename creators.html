What's worked for other apps

I went through interviews with the teams behind apps that grew through creators, and pulled out what's useful if you're the one making the videos. None of this has been tested on Ting yet, so treat it as a starting point and tell me what you find.

The first 3 seconds

  • Watch your 3-second retention. Nomad Table's founder looks for 75 to 80% of viewers still watching after 3 seconds, and treats it as the best early sign of a format that will go viral. (Nomad Table)
  • Do something in the hook. Show a reaction, a prop or a real emotion. The exact reaction matters less than doing something. (Nomad Table, Pingo)
  • Make the first frame look good. It's one of three things Jenny AI checks for in a creator, along with how expressive you are and how well you tell a story. (Jenny AI)
  • Deliver on the hook. One clickbait hook got about 300,000 views, but only 174 likes and 8 comments, because the video didn't pay it off. (Nomad Table)

Showing the app

  • Get the app in within 15 seconds. Cal AI called this probably one of its biggest rules. (Cal AI)
  • Show one clear moment. Cal AI's was taking a photo of food. For Ting, I'd show tapping a word and instantly seeing what it means.
  • Use the app, don't just mention it. Pingo found videos of people using the app converted far better than slideshows naming it on the last slide. (Pingo)
  • Make it feel like your normal content. A natural mention is the hard part, so don't read lines word for word. Natural doesn't mean hidden, though: always keep your paid partnership label on. (Cal AI, Sideshift)

Finding a format

  • Start from what's already working. Find videos doing well in your niche and change them by at least 10 to 20%, so it's your own take and not a copy. (Sideshift)
  • Look beyond app videos. Ordinary videos about a frustration can make great formats. Keep the problem and add your mention of the app. (Sideshift)
  • Try culture and humour. Pingo did well with beginner basics and with cultural hooks, like K-dramas and current events. (Pingo)
  • Say something people will debate. A take that divides opinion gets people replying. Aim for real disagreement, not pure rage bait. (Jenny AI)
  • Be early on new formats. The first videos in a new format are often the ones that go the most viral. (Sideshift)

Sticking with it

  • Warm up a new account. Start with a video made purely for views, with no mention of the app. It helps show the algorithm you're a real person and who your content is for. (Sideshift, Pingo)
  • Don't judge yourself on one video. One of Jenny AI's best creators took 25 videos to pass 10,000 views. Two near-identical videos from one creator, 3 days apart, were 2,000 times apart in views. (Jenny AI)
  • Look for repeat 10,000s. Several videos in one format passing 10,000 views is a good sign it has legs. (Sideshift)
  • Read your comments, not just your views. People asking what the app is or how to get it matters more than a big number. (Cal AI, Nomad Table)
  • When something works, tell me. I'll share what I learn with everyone making videos for Ting, so a winning format helps all of us.

Mostly from interviews on Superwall's podcast. The numbers are what each team reported, and I haven't checked them. Sideshift sells creator campaigns, so keep that in mind with its advice.
