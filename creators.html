1Who we're talking to

Ting is an iPhone app that teaches Mandarin through a feed of short, real Chinese clips. Each clip uses words the learner mostly knows, with one new word at a time. Tap any word for its pinyin and meaning. Every video speaks to one person.

The rusty middle learner

  • Learned Chinese before (school, uni, a class, an app), then stopped.
  • Remembers lots of words, but can't follow people talking at normal speed.
  • Dramas are too hard. Beginner videos are too easy and a bit boring. Nothing sits in the middle.
  • Thinks they don't have time for a TV show, but scrolls more than they'd like.
  • Wants about 15 minutes a day, and the feeling of "I got better at listening today".

In their own words

Use these lines. When they hear their own thought, they stop scrolling.

I know the words, I just can't hear them when people talk. Dramas are too fast and beginner videos put me to sleep. I used to be decent at Chinese. I want to know I'm actually getting better.

What we promise them: the missing middle. Real Chinese that's just hard enough, about 15 minutes a day. Never shame anyone for scrolling. Show them a better use of 15 minutes of it.

2The one rule: show the click

People download when they see the app working on a real phone, not when they're told about it. Every video, in every format, shows this moment:

A real clip playsSomeone speaking Chinese at normal speed, inside Ting.
You tap a wordThe pinyin and meaning pop up.
It clicksSuddenly you understand what they actually said.

Get to the app within 15 seconds. Screen-record on your iPhone (Control Centre, then Screen Recording) using your own Ting account, so it shows your real words and progress.

3What to film

Four formats that already work for Ting or for apps like it. Start with the first two, post a few of each, and we'll double down on whatever wins. Change anything so it sounds like you, and only say what's true for you.

3 to 12 sHard cut to your screen recording. A clip plays, you tap a word, the meaning shows, then a quiz you get right.

Why it works: it's cheap to film in batches, and the app is on screen by second three.

EndTing on your phone for a second or two.Say or type: "Comment how much you caught."

Why it works: it is exactly this person's problem. They know every word and still miss it at speed, and the replay shows them they were closer than they thought. The comments fill up with scores.

Why it works: versions of this for other language apps reached millions of views in 5 to 15 seconds. Go big with the feeling. Calm versions don't travel.

4Hooks to start with

The first three seconds decide everything. Pick one, type it as text in TikTok or Instagram, and make sure the video pays it off.

  1. You know the words. You just can't hear them.
  2. Beginner videos too easy, dramas too hard? This is the middle.
  3. Stopped learning Chinese? You remember more than you think.
  4. I used to study Chinese. Now I can't follow a single conversation.
  5. 15 minutes of this instead of Instagram, every day
  6. The step between Chinese cartoons and Chinese TV
  7. Can you follow this? Most people who studied Chinese can't.
  8. I wish I found this before I gave up on Chinese
  9. Wait, Chinese lessons can be YouTube clips?
  10. POV: every word in the video is tappable

5Do and don't

Do

  • Show the real app on your phone, every time.
  • Do something in the first second: a reaction, a prop, a real feeling.
  • Look into the camera on the hook, and keep it fast.
  • Add hook text inside TikTok or Instagram, not in an editor.
  • Use a sound that's trending right now.
  • End with "Search ‘Ting - Learn Chinese Fast’ on the App Store", or point to your bio link.
  • Label it as paid: TikTok's "Paid partnership" setting, Instagram's "Paid partnership" label, or #ad. It's the law in Australia and the US.

Don't

  • Promise fluency or speed, like "fluent in 3 months" or "10x faster".
  • Call Ting "free". It has a paid plan with a trial.
  • Burn text into the video in an editing app.
  • Show other apps' logos or put other apps down by name.
  • Offer anything in exchange for App Store ratings or reviews.
  • Download and repost the original YouTube videos behind Ting's clips. Show them inside Ting instead.
  • Film other people without their OK.

6How it works

You won't be left on your own. I find what's working across language-learning TikTok and send it to you, so you're never guessing.

  1. ApplySend your channels and your best-performing video.
  2. Try TingGet full Ting Pro access and use it for a few days. Real reactions make the best videos.
  3. Agree your offerWe agree your pay and video count together before you start.
  4. Get your kitYour own App Store link for your bio, screen recordings, and ready-made listening clips.

Your week

  • Batch film once or twice a week: several hooks and screen recordings in one go.
  • Post about once a day, when your audience is most active.
  • Send me each link. You'll get feedback within 24 hours: what to keep, what to cut, which hook to try next.
  • A 15-minute call every two weeks to look at what's working and pick the next formats.
  • When a video wins, remake it 3 to 5 times with a new hook or a new clip. Winning formats usually stay hot for a week or two.

What we look at

  • Downloads from your link, and comments asking about the app. A video with 10,000 views and 100 downloads beats a million views and none.
  • 3-second hold. If most people are still watching after 3 seconds, the hook works.
  • Not one video. One creator at another app took 25 videos to pass 10,000 views. Several videos in one format passing 10,000 is the sign to keep going.
  • New account? Warm it up for 5 to 7 days first: watch, like and comment on Chinese-learning videos, then start posting.
