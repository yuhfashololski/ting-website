1. Learn from videos you actually enjoy

Textbook dialogues are boring, and real Chinese TV is way too fast to follow.

Scroll through short clips from Chinese creators: gaming, vlogs, cooking, dramas, stories and podcasts. Each one is picked so you already know almost every word.

2. Understand every word instantly

One unknown word and the whole sentence is gone. So you avoid real Chinese, and stay stuck as a beginner.

Every line is laid out word by word with pinyin above. Tap any word to see:

  • Its meaning in this sentence
  • Its tones
  • An example from another real clip
  • Saved straight to your flashcards

3. Remember what you learn

You look a word up, and two days later it's gone.

Every word you tap becomes a flashcard from the clip you heard it in. Ting brings it back just before you'd forget it, in a fresh clip, scheduled by a modern spaced-repetition model (FSRS). Review feels like watching, not studying.

Meet Ting

听 (tīng) means listen. That's the whole idea: you learn Chinese the way you learnt your first language, by hearing real people use it, at a level you can follow.

Starting from zero? Ting teaches your first words, then finds real clips made almost entirely of them. Already know some Chinese? Your first few clips double as a quick level check.

Over 10,000 clips across HSK 1–6, each a few seconds long, with about one new word at a time and a picture that shows you what it means.

Good to know

How much does Ting cost?

Ting Pro is US$49.99 a year, with a 7-day free trial, or US$9.99 a month. The app shows the price in your currency before you confirm. Cancel any time in Settings → your name → Subscriptions. The videos themselves are YouTube's, and always free to watch on YouTube.

Do I need to know any Chinese?

No. Ting starts beginners with their first words and real clips made of them. If you already know some, your first clips fine-tune your level.

Where do the videos come from?

From Chinese creators on YouTube, played through YouTube's official embedded player. Ting never copies or hosts any video, and every clip links back to its creator.

Which devices does it work on?

iPhone and iPad. Watching clips needs an internet connection; your saved words and reviews work offline.
